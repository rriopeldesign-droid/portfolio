Roadside Assistance Portal

Helped rebuild the portal our 24/7 roadside partner used, so their agents could see each booking’s coverage instead of calling us to ask.

Context

RVezy partners with Access Roadside, a Canadian company providing 24/7 roadside coverage. Their agents handled RVezy cases through a portal connected to our internal operations dashboard.

This was early in my time at RVezy, when I led roadside operations.

Role

Led roadside operations; designed and shipped the fix

Scope

Roadside dashboard in our internal ops system, partner permissions

Team

Me and one engineer, who reviewed the code and kept the backend in sync

Partner

Access Roadside, 24/7 roadside coverage

The Problem

Every unclear case became a phone call

There were constant disputes over what was covered. Coverage depended on the insurance tier each renter chose at booking: tire replacement, for example, was covered on some bookings and not others.

The portal didn’t show which tier applied to the booking an agent was working on. So every unclear case became a phone call.

Research

The calls weren’t about the repair

Because I led roadside operations, I handled cases directly with renters broken down on the side of the road. Access Roadside’s service providers also called my cell about specific cases.

The pattern was clear. Most calls weren’t about the repair. They were about whether that booking covered it.

50%+

of roadside cases had some sort of dispute

where Customer Success (me) had to get involved

The Decision

From a guide to the actual data

The first instinct was to send Access Roadside a clearer coverage guide, such as a PDF or FAQ explaining each tier. But agents already understood the tiers in general. What they lacked was which tier this booking had.

So the fix moved from writing a guide to showing each booking’s coverage in the portal.

Solution

A dashboard for roadside, and the right permissions

We rebuilt the portal in two parts.

A new roadside dashboard

A dashboard for the roadside team inside our internal ops system, built around the booking an agent is working on.

Revised partner permissions

Access Roadside agents could see each booking’s specific coverage without reaching the rest of the system.

Design Constraints

Not a visual redesign

Internal tools followed an existing Google-based style guide, so this wasn’t a visual redesign. The design work was information hierarchy: what an agent needs to see first, with a renter stranded at the roadside, to make a coverage call without phoning us.

My Role

From the phone calls to the code

I led roadside operations, identified the problem, and shipped the new dashboard and the permission changes. One other engineer worked alongside me, reviewing the code and making sure the backend stayed in sync.

After Launch

Contact between Customer Success and roadside, cut in half

What we measured

Back-and-forth between Customer Success and roadside dropped significantly. Contact between the two teams was cut in half.

What we learned

The disputes were a data problem, not a knowledge problem. Agents already knew the tiers. Once they could see which tier a booking had, they could make the call themselves.

What it didn’t fix

Billing. Access Roadside still emailed us an invoice for every ticket they handled.

Reflection

What I’d do differently

Fix billing at the same time. Access Roadside emailed us an invoice for every ticket they handled. I’d have built a way for them to submit invoices into our system and for finance to review them there.

I raised it, others did too, and engineering built it after I’d moved off the team.
